# REVIEW-2026-10-07 — the scoped cold review of the receiver series' close

Scope: `63cbcca..86183b0` on `receiver` in `~/git/spanweave-live`, thirteen commits
— seven code/docs commits and six `plan:` commits — reviewed under the §0.2 aux
protocol (`git show 63cbcca:WORKPLAN.md`, now registered in `TASKS.md`).

One sub-agent per code commit, each cold, each in a worktree created by absolute
path under the session scratchpad, plus one hygiene agent for the seven plan
commits. Parents were **derived** (`git rev-parse <sha>^`), never taken from a
sha a commit body or a document names. Because every code commit's derived parent
is a `plan:` commit, a **mutation the new tests catch** was required for all
seven, not only a red-on-parent run.

No tracked file was edited and nothing was committed by this review or by any of
its sub-agents. Every finding below is for the builder to make into a commit.

**This review decides whether `receiver` merges to `main`.**

---

## Verdict

**The code is merge-ready. The record is not yet true, and eight things block.**

Every one of the seven code commits passes the protocol: the spec was edited in
the same commit as the behaviour, a mutation was shown caught, the injected-seam
rules hold, `make check` is green at each, and the CI matrix is green 7/7 on the
tip. The series' central claims were **independently reproduced** rather than
taken on report — gate A's byte-for-byte equality, R8's first-failure table (by
two independent paths), R6's endpoint behaviour on a real socket, and R7's seam
allowlist literal.

What blocks is the **record**: `TASKS.md` is the permanent registry this series
built to outlive its plan, and eight statements in it, in `SPEC.md` and in three
modules under `spanweave_live/` are false at the tip. None of them is a code
defect. All eight are one follow-up commit of prose, with no change to behaviour
and no change to a test. They block because this is the one artifact a later
reader cannot repair from the code: it *is* the account.

That is also this series' own standard, applied to itself. `WORKPLAN.md` §0.1
step 5 made a wrong criterion somebody's job — *"a wrong criterion has an owner,
and it is this step"* — and the series struck through its own premises six times
on that rule. Review 1 registered **P-3** (a factor of two) and review 2
registered **T9** (an unreproducible 69/69) for exactly this class, with T9's own
disposition saying *"the series' credibility rests on its numbers being
re-derivable."* Four of the numbers below do not re-derive.

## 2. Threads — true, recorded, do not block

### The two with behaviour behind them (both R6, `spanweave_live/endpoint.py`)

- **T-R6-4 — `serve` re-yields the previous request's `Records`.** When
  `handle_request()` handles nothing, the generator yields the prior request's
  object again. Reachable from outside by a bare TCP connect-and-close; the
  `requests` count stays 1 and nothing is counted. Mitigated only by
  double-routing being graph-idempotent, which is luck rather than design.
- **T-R6-5 — a client that aborts before reading leaks a `socketserver`
  traceback to stderr with `counts == {}`.** The records still reach the
  consumer, so nothing is dropped and standing rule 5 is not broken in substance
  — but an uncounted traceback on the error stream is the shape that rule exists
  to prevent, and the endpoint's own `400` path does this correctly.

These are the highest-value threads in the review: they are the only two findings
anywhere in the seven commits that are about behaviour rather than prose.
